Coursework Integrity & AI Policy (MO-SS033-TCW)

BALANCING THE SCALES:
Where AI is in Education

Generative AI has made it easy for senior high school students to hand their coursework over to a tool. This presentation examines how misuse affects learning and proposes three structured solutions to keep AI in the classroom without sacrificing critical thinking.

Group 3 Position Statement

"AI should be integrated into education through clear policies and responsible use rather than total bans, so it supports learning without replacing students' own thinking."

The Problem

How GenAI Transformed Student Coursework

Research Study
Highlight of the Decade

ChatGPT & Senior High Usage

Since ChatGPT's late-2022 release, generative AI has enabled students to shortcut critical thinking or cheat.

Iriga Research Findings:

Students primarily use AI for writing essays and assignments, followed by enhancing reports and presentations.

Cognitive Impact
Bypassing Active Learning

AI Outsourcing

Tasks exist to reinforce classroom theory. Offloading thinking to machines bypasses active learning and weakens core concept application.

  • Loss of analytical and problem-solving skills
  • Reduced face-to-face social interactions
  • Deterioration of interpersonal skills
Social Dynamics
Chatbots & Confidants

Gateway for Emotional Support

Younger generations display high openness to AI chatbots, seeking neutral, judgment-free confidants outside of academic settings.

Critical Consequences:

Risk of social isolation when AI replaces human connection, alongside data privacy, safety, and environmental concerns.

Geopolitical Landscape

Technological Zeitgeist & Global Alignment

AI is not just a classroom tool—it is a critical national economic and geopolitical asset.

China & US AI Strategies

China unveiled a 10-year plan to lead global AI development. Concurrently, the U.S. government recently barred the use of Claude's Fable and Mythos AI models due to national security concerns. These geopolitical actions shape tool accessibility, safety, and credibility worldwide.

Policies: Global Leaders vs Philippines (DepEd)

US & China Paradigm

Heavy integration, AI-focused curricula, and strong industry-school partnerships designed to train students to build future AI technology.

Philippines (DepEd) Policy

Permits controlled AI usage, positioning AI as a guided assistant rather than a replacement. Focuses on supervised collaboration.

Stakeholder Analysis

Perspectives Across Education

Select a stakeholder group to view their challenges and opportunities.

Frontline Challenge

Educators

Educators occupy the frontline of AI integration, facing the challenge of balancing pedagogical benefits against instructional integrity. While teachers recognize AI's potential as a supportive tool, there is deep concern over its ability to erode original student thinking and academic discipline.

Key Goal: Navigating 21st-century digital learning strategies to ensure AI tools complement engagement without replacing core learning processes.
Double-Edged Tool

Senior High School Students

Structured engagement with AI can enhance cognitive skills, problem-solving capabilities, and digital self-efficacy. However, utilizing AI to execute tasks directly—rather than for early brainstorming or language refinement—risks causing cognitive laziness and academic dishonesty.

Impact: Over-reliance diminishes writing self-efficacy and fosters habits that reduce personal effort in learning.
Governance & Equity

Academic Institutions

Leadership focuses on governance, policy, and educational equity across the learning continuum. Structured institutional policies guiding responsible, limited AI usage show promise when executed properly to maintain academic integrity.

Equity Focus: Safeguarding learning outcomes across secondary and tertiary settings without widening academic or digital divides.
Public Perception

AI Through the Media's Lens

Media coverage displays a mixed reception. While AI is portrayed as a helpful assistant for brainstorming and research, Philippine educators and journalists highlight serious concerns regarding unmonitored out-of-classroom assessments.

Primary Risk: Unmonitored out-of-classroom AI usage risks reducing opportunities for students to build critical problem-solving skills independently.

Actionable Frameworks

Our Three Proposed Solutions

Pragmatic approaches that preserve critical thinking while embracing innovation.

Solution 01

AI Assessment Scale (AIAS)

Instead of banning AI outright, the AIAS trains students in responsible, healthy usage across 5 clear levels.

9-Month Implementation | 6-Month Evaluation
Level 1: No AI
1. No AI 2. Planning 3. Collaboration 4. Full AI 5. Exploration

Level 1: No AI

Tasks completed entirely by the student without any AI generation or assistance. Used for foundational assessments, exams, and baseline critical thinking exercises.

Teacher Evaluation Primary Focus: 100% Original Human Work

3 Policy Implementation Stages:

Stage 1: Design

Hire policy consultants, commissions, or think tanks alongside educational domain experts.

Stage 2: Pilot

Implement the pilot program in volunteer secondary schools to measure practical classroom adoption.

Stage 3: Revision

Refine policies based on direct feedback from educators and students after the test period.

Known AIAS Implementation Challenges:

Students may still attempt dishonest AI use; teachers with low technical skills may struggle initially without guidance; some schools may opt for total bans initially before seeing safe ethical usage outcomes nationwide.

Solution 02

AI Literacy Education Program

An 8-session, 12-hour structured program teaching secondary students how AI algorithms work, prompt ethics, critical questioning, and hands-on tool usage.

Evidence & Impact:

Teaches students to question AI outputs rather than accepting them blindly. In a Tehran study of 9th graders, trained students gained significant critical thinking ability (effect size 0.629).

Solution 03

UNESCO's Guidance for GenAI

UNESCO's first global policy guidance advises governments and schools to regulate generative AI ethically rather than leaving it unmonitored or implementing total bans.

Regulatory Safeguards:

Enforces data privacy protections, age limits, and validation of AI tools before classroom entry—ensuring human teachers maintain primary control over learning evaluations.

Final Reflection

Balancing Innovation and Preservation

"While AI can support research and learning, its misuse in completing coursework may weaken critical thinking, creativity, problem-solving skills, and academic integrity. Both sides of the issue have fair points—which means an approach addressing the painpoints of both is most ideal."

Innovate not just for the sake of improvement, but for our future as well. For what do technological advancements mean when our people and our future will bear the outcome of our decisions?
